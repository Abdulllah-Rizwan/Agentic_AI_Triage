Basic Emergency First Aid — Pakistan Disaster Context

This guide covers immediate first aid actions for the most common emergencies in disaster-affected areas of Pakistan. In any life-threatening emergency, call Rescue 1122 (Punjab), Edhi Foundation 115, or Pakistan Red Crescent 1716.

UNRESPONSIVE PERSON — CHECK AND ACT

If you find someone unresponsive:
Shout for help. Check if they respond to your voice or a tap on the shoulder.
Look, listen, and feel for breathing for no more than 10 seconds.
If not breathing normally, begin CPR immediately.
If breathing, place them in the recovery position: roll them onto their side, tilt head back slightly to keep airway open, and stay with them until help arrives.

CPR FOR AN ADULT (Hands-Only)

If the person is not breathing:
Place the heel of one hand on the center of the chest (lower half of breastbone).
Place the other hand on top, fingers interlocked and raised off the chest.
Push straight down hard and fast — at least 5 cm deep, at a rate of 100–120 times per minute.
Do not stop until emergency services arrive or the person begins to breathe normally.
Hands-only CPR is effective and safe even without formal training. Do not delay it.

SEVERE BLEEDING

Apply firm, continuous pressure to the wound using a clean cloth, clothing, or bandage.
Do not remove the cloth — if blood soaks through, add more material on top.
If possible, raise the injured area above the level of the heart.
Do not remove large embedded objects (knives, glass, metal) — apply padding around them and press beside them.
For limb bleeding that cannot be controlled, apply a tourniquet 5 cm above the wound, tighten until bleeding stops, and note the time it was applied. Tell medical staff.
Maintain pressure until emergency help arrives.

CHOKING — ADULT OR CHILD OVER 1 YEAR

If a person is coughing forcefully, encourage them to keep coughing.
If they cannot cough, speak, or breathe, give up to 5 sharp back blows between shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
If back blows fail, give up to 5 abdominal thrusts: stand behind them, place a fist just above the navel, grasp with the other hand, and pull sharply inward and upward.
Alternate 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until the object dislodges or the person loses consciousness.
If they lose consciousness, begin CPR.

BURNS

Cool the burn immediately under cool (not ice cold) running water for at least 20 minutes.
Remove clothing and jewelry near the burn unless stuck to the skin — do not pull stuck material.
Cover loosely with a clean non-fluffy cloth or cling film.
Do not apply toothpaste, butter, oil, or any home remedies — these trap heat and cause infection.
Seek emergency care for: burns larger than the palm of the hand, burns on the face, hands, genitals, or feet, burns caused by chemicals or electricity, or burns in children.

HEATSTROKE (EMERGENCY)

Heatstroke is life-threatening. Body temperature rises above 40°C (104°F). Signs include: hot, dry skin; confusion or loss of consciousness; rapid breathing; no sweating despite extreme heat.

Move the person immediately to shade or a cool area.
Cool them rapidly: pour cool water over their body, fan them vigorously, apply wet cloths to neck, armpits, and groin.
Do not give fluids to an unconscious person.
Get emergency help immediately — heatstroke can cause brain damage within minutes.

NOSEBLEED

Sit the person upright and have them lean slightly forward (not backward).
Pinch the soft part of the nose firmly, just below the bony bridge.
Hold continuously for 10–15 minutes without releasing.
Breathe through the mouth.
If bleeding continues after 20 minutes, or if caused by a head injury, seek emergency care immediately.

SUSPECTED FRACTURE (BROKEN BONE)

Do not try to straighten or push the bone back.
Immobilize the injury in the position found using a splint (a rigid object — board, stick) padded with cloth.
For an arm: tie the splint to the arm, then support it in a sling.
For a leg: tie it to the other leg or to a long padded board.
If a neck or spine injury is suspected (from fall, vehicle accident, or collapse), do not move the person unless they are in immediate danger. Keep the head, neck, and spine aligned.
Seek emergency care.

DROWNING / NEAR-DROWNING

Ensure your own safety before entering water — reach or throw rather than entering.
Once the person is out of the water and unresponsive: check for breathing.
Begin CPR immediately if not breathing — do not wait for water to drain.
Even if the person seems to recover, they must be taken to hospital — secondary drowning can occur hours later.

SEIZURE

Do not restrain the person or hold their limbs down.
Clear the area of hard or sharp objects.
Place something soft under their head.
Turn them gently onto their side if possible.
Do not put anything in their mouth.
Time the seizure. If it lasts more than 5 minutes, or the person does not regain consciousness, call for emergency help immediately.
After the seizure, keep them on their side (recovery position) until fully conscious.

WHEN TO CALL FOR HELP IMMEDIATELY

Call Rescue 1122 / Edhi 115 for any of the following:
Unconsciousness or unresponsiveness
Chest pain or difficulty breathing
Severe or uncontrolled bleeding
Suspected stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech)
Snake or animal bite
Seizure lasting more than 5 minutes
Heatstroke
Suspected poisoning
Suspected spinal injury
Severe burn
