Basic Emergency First Aid — Pakistan Disaster Context

TOPIC: first aid, basic first aid, emergency, CPR, unconscious, choking, burns, fracture, seizure, nosebleed

RECOGNIZING COMMON EMERGENCIES:
- Unresponsive person: not reacting to voice or touch, not breathing normally
- Choking: cannot speak, cough, or breathe; may clutch throat; face turning red or blue
- Severe burn: large area, deep, on face/hands/genitals, or caused by chemical/electricity
- Fracture: pain at specific point, swelling, deformity, inability to move limb normally
- Heatstroke: hot dry skin, confusion, no sweating despite extreme heat, temperature above 40°C
- Nosebleed: continuous bleeding from the nose
- Seizure: sudden shaking and loss of consciousness

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Call Rescue 1122 / Edhi 115 immediately for:
- Unconsciousness or unresponsiveness
- Chest pain or difficulty breathing
- Severe or uncontrolled bleeding
- Suspected stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech)
- Snake or animal bite
- Seizure lasting more than 5 minutes
- Heatstroke
- Suspected poisoning
- Suspected spinal injury
- Severe burn

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:

Unresponsive person:
1. Shout for help. Check for response (voice and tap on shoulder).
2. Look, listen, and feel for breathing for no more than 10 seconds.
3. If not breathing normally — begin CPR immediately.
4. If breathing — place in recovery position: roll onto side, tilt head back to keep airway open.

CPR for an adult (hands-only):
1. Place heel of one hand on centre of chest (lower half of breastbone). Place other hand on top, fingers interlocked.
2. Push straight down hard and fast — at least 5 cm deep, 100–120 times per minute.
3. Do not stop until emergency help arrives or person begins breathing normally.

Choking (adult or child over 1 year):
1. If coughing forcefully — encourage them to keep coughing.
2. If cannot cough, speak, or breathe: give up to 5 sharp back blows between shoulder blades.
3. If back blows fail: give up to 5 abdominal thrusts (stand behind, fist above navel, pull sharply inward and upward).
4. Alternate 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts. If they lose consciousness, begin CPR.

Severe bleeding:
1. Apply firm continuous pressure with a clean cloth. Do not remove it — add more on top if soaked.
2. Raise the injured area above heart level.
3. For limb bleeding not controlled with pressure: apply tourniquet 5 cm above wound, note the time.

Burns:
1. Cool under cool (not ice cold) running water for at least 20 minutes.
2. Remove clothing and jewellery near the burn unless stuck to skin.
3. Cover loosely with clean non-fluffy cloth or cling film.
4. Do not apply toothpaste, butter, oil, or any home remedies.

Heatstroke:
1. Move immediately to shade or cool area.
2. Pour cool water over body, fan vigorously, apply wet cloths to neck, armpits, and groin.
3. Do not give fluids to an unconscious person.

Nosebleed:
1. Sit upright, lean slightly forward.
2. Pinch soft part of nose firmly for 10–15 minutes without releasing.
3. If bleeding continues after 20 minutes or caused by head injury — seek emergency care.

Fracture (broken bone):
1. Do not try to straighten or push bone back.
2. Immobilize in position found using a padded rigid splint.
3. For arm: splint + sling. For leg: tie to other leg or padded board.
4. If neck or spine injury suspected — do not move unless in immediate danger.

Seizure:
1. Do not restrain. Clear area of hard objects, place soft support under head.
2. Turn gently onto their side.
3. Do not put anything in their mouth.
4. Time the seizure. If longer than 5 minutes — call for emergency help immediately.
5. After seizure: keep on side until fully conscious.

DO NOT:
- Do not delay CPR — hands-only CPR is safe even without formal training.
- Do not apply toothpaste, butter, or home remedies to burns — they trap heat and cause infection.
- Do not try to realign broken bones.
- Do not put anything in the mouth of a person having a seizure.
