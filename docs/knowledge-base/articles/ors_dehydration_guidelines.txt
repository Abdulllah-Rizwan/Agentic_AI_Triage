Dehydration and Oral Rehydration Therapy — Practical Guide

Dehydration is the most immediate life-threatening consequence of diarrhoea, vomiting, and heat exposure in disaster-affected communities in Pakistan. Children under 5 can die from dehydration within hours of severe diarrhoea onset. Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) saves lives — it is the first and most important treatment in the field before hospital care is available.

RECOGNIZING DEHYDRATION

Mild dehydration: thirst, dry mouth, slightly less urine than normal, fatigue.

Moderate dehydration (needs prompt ORS treatment): sunken eyes, dry mouth and lips, no tears when crying (in children), reduced urination (dark yellow urine), skin that is slow to return to normal when pinched (skin tenting), headache, dizziness when standing.

Severe dehydration — medical emergency: very sunken eyes, no urine output for 6+ hours, extreme weakness or inability to stand, rapid weak pulse, cold hands and feet, lethargy or loss of consciousness, in infants: sunken fontanelle (soft spot on top of head). Intravenous fluids are required immediately.

HOW TO MAKE ORS AT HOME

Standard WHO ORS packet: dissolve one packet in 1 litre of clean water (boiled and cooled, or bottled water with unbroken seal). Stir well.

If ORS packets are not available, prepare a home solution:
1 litre of clean water
6 level teaspoons of sugar (granulated)
half a level teaspoon of salt

Stir until completely dissolved. Taste it — it should taste like slightly salty tears. If it tastes saltier than tears, add more water. Never add extra salt — too much salt worsens dehydration.

Do not add sugar without salt, or salt without sugar — both are needed.

GIVING ORS

Adults and children over 2 years: give small sips frequently. After each loose stool, give an adult 200–400 ml ORS, a child 100–200 ml.
Children under 2 years: give 50–100 ml ORS after each loose stool, using a spoon or syringe. Do not give large volumes at once.
Infants under 6 months: continue breastfeeding as frequently as possible, and give additional ORS 50 ml after each stool.

If the child vomits after ORS: wait 10 minutes, then restart very slowly — 5 ml (one teaspoon) every minute. This usually stays down.

Continue ORS until diarrhoea stops and the child is urinating normally.

WHEN ORS IS NOT ENOUGH — SEEK EMERGENCY CARE

ORS cannot treat severe dehydration. Take the patient to hospital immediately if:
The patient is too weak or drowsy to drink.
Vomiting is so frequent that ORS cannot be kept down.
Signs of severe dehydration (see above).
Diarrhoea contains blood.
Symptoms are not improving or are worsening after 3–4 hours of ORS.
The patient is a young infant (under 3 months) with any diarrhoea and vomiting.

In hospital, dehydration is treated with intravenous fluids (Ringer's lactate or normal saline). For children in severe dehydration: 100 ml/kg IV fluid over 3 hours (WHO standard protocol).

FEEDING DURING DIARRHOEA

Continue feeding children throughout diarrhoea — do not stop food. Starvation weakens the child further.
Breastfeeding must continue without interruption.
Give simple, easily digestible foods: rice, dal, khichri, bananas, boiled potatoes.
Avoid fatty, sugary, or spicy food.
Resume normal diet as soon as the child's appetite returns.

ZINC SUPPLEMENTATION

WHO recommends zinc supplementation for all children under 5 with diarrhoea:
Children under 6 months: 10 mg zinc daily for 10–14 days.
Children 6 months to 5 years: 20 mg zinc daily for 10–14 days.
Zinc reduces the duration of diarrhoea by 25% and reduces the risk of future episodes.

ORS + ZINC together are the two-part treatment that saves children's lives from diarrhoeal disease.

CHOLERA-SPECIFIC REHYDRATION

In cholera, fluid loss through diarrhoea is extremely rapid — adults can lose 1 litre per hour. ORS may not keep pace.
Administer ORS continuously and aggressively.
All suspected cholera patients should be taken to a health facility for assessment and antibiotic treatment.
Oral cholera vaccine is available during outbreaks through NDMA and WHO emergency health response.
