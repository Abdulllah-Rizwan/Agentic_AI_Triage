Dehydration and Oral Rehydration Therapy — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: dehydration, ORS, oral rehydration, pani ki kami, fluid loss, rehydration

RECOGNIZING DEHYDRATION:
Mild: thirst, dry mouth, slightly less urine, fatigue.

Moderate (needs prompt ORS treatment): sunken eyes, dry mouth and lips, no tears when crying (in children), dark yellow urine, skin slow to return when pinched, headache, dizziness when standing.

Severe (medical emergency — needs IV fluids): very sunken eyes, no urine for 6+ hours, extreme weakness or inability to stand, rapid weak pulse, cold hands and feet, lethargy or loss of consciousness, in infants: sunken fontanelle (soft spot on top of head).

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Take the patient to hospital immediately if:
- Too weak or drowsy to drink
- Vomiting so frequent that ORS cannot be kept down
- Signs of severe dehydration (see above)
- Diarrhoea contains blood
- Symptoms not improving or worsening after 3–4 hours of ORS
- A young infant (under 3 months) with any diarrhoea and vomiting

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:

How to make ORS at home:
- Standard WHO packet: dissolve 1 packet in 1 litre of clean (boiled and cooled) water.
- If no packets: 1 litre clean water + 6 level teaspoons sugar + half a level teaspoon salt. Stir until dissolved. It should taste like slightly salty tears. Never add extra salt.

Giving ORS:
1. Adults and children over 2 years: give small sips frequently. After each loose stool — 200–400 ml for adults, 100–200 ml for children.
2. Children under 2 years: give 50–100 ml ORS after each stool using a spoon or syringe.
3. Infants under 6 months: continue breastfeeding frequently and give 50 ml ORS after each stool.
4. If the child vomits after ORS: wait 10 minutes, then restart very slowly — 5 ml (one teaspoon) every minute.
5. Continue ORS until diarrhoea stops and the child is urinating normally.

Feeding during diarrhoea:
1. Continue feeding children throughout diarrhoea — do not stop food. Starvation weakens the child further.
2. Breastfeeding must continue without interruption.
3. Give simple foods: rice, dal, khichri, bananas, boiled potatoes.
4. Avoid fatty, sugary, or spicy food.

For children under 5 with diarrhoea, give zinc tablets if available:
- Children under 6 months: 10 mg daily for 10–14 days.
- Children 6 months to 5 years: 20 mg daily for 10–14 days.
Zinc reduces diarrhoea duration by 25% and reduces risk of future episodes.

DO NOT:
- Do not add extra salt to ORS — too much salt worsens dehydration.
- Do not give fruit juice or fizzy drinks — they worsen diarrhoea.
- Do not stop ORS if the patient is vomiting — reduce amount, give more frequently.
- Do not stop feeding during diarrhoea.
