Pneumonia — Recognition and Field Management

RECOGNIZING PNEUMONIA:
- Cough with fast or difficult breathing
- Fever (often high, above 38.5°C)
- Chest pain that worsens when breathing deeply or coughing
- Bluish lips or fingernails (severe — needs immediate hospital)
- Child breathing very fast: over 50 breaths/minute under 1 year; over 40 breaths/minute aged 1–5
- Lower chest indrawing in children: chest wall sucks inward during breathing (not outward)
- Wheezing or grunting sounds when breathing

SEVERITY ASSESSMENT:
Mild (can treat with antibiotics at home if prescribed):
- Breathing fast but no chest indrawing
- Able to drink fluids
- No blue lips

Severe (send to hospital now):
- Chest indrawing visible
- Cannot drink or breastfeed
- Blue lips or fingernails
- Convulsions
- Lethargic or unconscious

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING FOR MEDICAL CARE:
1. Keep the patient sitting upright or with head elevated — lying flat worsens breathing.
2. Loosen tight clothing around the chest and neck.
3. Keep the patient warm.
4. Give paracetamol for fever (do not give aspirin to children).
5. Encourage small sips of fluid frequently — do not force large amounts.
6. If prescribed amoxicillin by a health worker, give the full course (5 days) even if the patient improves.
7. Do NOT give cough suppressants — coughing helps clear the airway.

DO NOT:
- Delay seeking care if breathing becomes difficult or fast — pneumonia in children kills within hours.
- Give antibiotics without medical advice for a first episode without assessment.
- Leave a child alone who has very fast breathing or chest indrawing.

GO TO HOSPITAL IMMEDIATELY IF:
- Child has blue lips, severe chest indrawing, or cannot drink
- Patient is confused or unconscious
- High fever with very fast breathing in any age
- No improvement after 2 days on prescribed antibiotics
- Any oxygen saturation below 90% if a pulse oximeter is available
