Fractures — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: fracture, broken bone, hadi tooti, broken leg, broken arm, spinal injury, dislocation

RECOGNIZING A FRACTURE:
Suspect a fracture when any of the following are present after an injury:
- Pain at a specific point, worsening with movement or pressure
- Swelling and bruising around the injury
- Visible deformity — the limb looks bent, shortened, or out of alignment
- Inability to move the affected limb normally
- A crack or snap was heard at the time of injury
- Bone visible through the skin (open/compound fracture — emergency)

Critical fractures that cause life-threatening internal bleeding:
- Femur (thigh bone): can trap 1–2 litres of blood. Patient shows shock signs: pale, cold, sweating, rapid weak pulse.
- Pelvis fracture: massive uncontrolled internal bleeding. Signs: hip and lower back pain, inability to walk, shock.
- Spine (neck or back): any fall from height, crushing injury, or high-speed trauma.

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
- All open fractures (bone through skin) — urgent, infection risk is immediate
- Femur or pelvis fractures — life-threatening internal bleeding
- Any fracture with signs of shock (pale, cold, confused, rapid weak pulse)
- Suspected spinal fracture (numbness, tingling, or inability to move limbs)
- Any fracture in a child
- Joint dislocations — do not attempt to reduce in the field

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:

General fracture immobilization:
1. Immobilize the fracture in the position found. Do not try to straighten or realign bones.
2. The splint must extend above and below the fracture, immobilizing joints on both sides.
3. Pad splints with cloth or clothing to prevent pressure sores.
4. Secure splints firmly but not so tight as to cut off circulation.
5. After splinting: check circulation — can the patient feel their fingers or toes? If the limb goes cold or numb, loosen the splint.

Arm fracture:
1. Use any rigid material as a splint (wooden board, thick cardboard, folded magazines).
2. Pad and tie it along the length of the arm.
3. Make a sling: fold large cloth into a triangle, place under arm, tie around neck. Elbow at 90 degrees.

Leg fracture:
1. Lower leg: use two padded boards on either side, or tie injured leg to uninjured leg with cloth strips above and below the fracture.
2. Thigh (femur): requires a long splint from armpit to foot. Keep patient lying flat. Monitor for shock.
3. Do not allow the patient to walk on a fractured leg.

Open fracture (bone through skin):
1. Do not push the bone back in.
2. Do not probe or clean inside the wound.
3. Cover the wound with a clean, damp cloth to keep it moist.
4. Immobilize the limb in the position found.
5. Get to hospital urgently — open fractures become infected rapidly.

Suspected spinal fracture:
1. Do not move the person unless they are in immediate danger (fire, rising water).
2. Hold the head still in the position found using both hands. Keep head, neck, and spine in a line.
3. If they must be moved: log-roll technique — one person holds the head while 2–3 others roll the body as a single unit onto a rigid board.

DO NOT:
- Do not try to straighten or realign fractured bones.
- Do not wrap splints too tightly — check circulation after splinting.
- Do not attempt to pop a dislocated joint back in — this requires medical expertise and incorrect attempts can fracture bones or trap nerves.
- Do not allow a person with a suspected femur or pelvis fracture to stand or walk.
