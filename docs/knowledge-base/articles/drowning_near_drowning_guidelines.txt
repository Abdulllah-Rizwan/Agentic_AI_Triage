Drowning and Near-Drowning — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: drowning, near drowning, submersion, flood drowning, child in water

RECOGNIZING DROWNING AND SECONDARY DROWNING:
Active drowning: person struggling in water, arms out to sides, head back, mouth at water level. Children may simply go quiet — they cannot call for help while drowning.

After rescue — secondary drowning warning signs (appear 1–24 hours after the incident even if the person walked away seemingly fine):
- Increasing difficulty breathing or persistent coughing
- Extreme fatigue or persistent sleepiness
- Confusion or unusual behaviour
- Chest pain
- Bluish tinge to the lips or skin

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Take any near-drowning victim to hospital — even if they appear fully recovered and are walking, talking, and breathing normally. Secondary drowning can occur 1–24 hours after submersion and can kill a child or adult who seemed fine. Inform the hospital that submersion occurred.

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:

Rescuing a drowning person — rescuer safety first:
Do not jump into fast-moving or deep water unless you are a trained swimmer. More rescuers die attempting untrained water rescues than victims.
1. REACH — extend a hand, rope, towel, branch, or clothing from shore without entering water.
2. THROW — throw a rope, life ring, empty jerrycan, or any buoyant object.
3. WADE — if water is shallow and calm, wade toward victim using a stick for support.
4. ROW — if a boat is available, row to the victim.
5. GO — enter water only as last resort if you are a confident swimmer. Approach from behind.

After removing from water:
1. Check responsiveness: call their name, tap shoulders.
2. If unresponsive and not breathing: begin CPR immediately. Do NOT waste time trying to drain water from lungs — CPR is the priority.
3. If breathing: place in recovery position (on their side, head tilted back). Monitor breathing continuously.

CPR for near-drowning (rescue breaths required — unlike cardiac arrest):
1. Give 5 initial rescue breaths before starting chest compressions.
2. Then continue: 30 chest compressions followed by 2 rescue breaths.
3. Compress hard and fast — at least 5 cm deep, 100–120 per minute.
4. Rescue breathing: tilt head back, lift chin, pinch nose closed, give a full breath watching chest rise.
5. Continue until the person breathes normally or emergency help arrives.

For hypothermia after cold floodwater:
1. Remove wet clothing immediately.
2. Wrap in blankets, including the head.
3. Do not rub limbs vigorously — rewarm gently.
4. Give warm sweet drinks only if person is fully conscious and not vomiting.

DO NOT:
- Do not enter fast-moving or deep water without rescue equipment.
- Do not try to drain water from the lungs before CPR — this wastes critical time.
- Do not assume a near-drowning victim is fine because they appear recovered — secondary drowning is real and dangerous.

PREVENTION DURING FLOODS:
- Never cross floodwater on foot without checking depth and speed — moving water above knee height is dangerous for adults.
- Keep children away from all flood-affected water bodies, ditches, and drains.
- Do not allow children to play near waterlogged areas — open drains and pits may be invisible under murky floodwater.
