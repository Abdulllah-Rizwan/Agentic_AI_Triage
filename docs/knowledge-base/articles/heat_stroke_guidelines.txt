Heat Exhaustion and Heatstroke — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: heatstroke, heat stroke, heat exhaustion, overheating, garmi, garmi ki bemari, hyperthermia

RECOGNIZING HEAT-RELATED ILLNESS — THREE STAGES:

Stage 1 — Heat Cramps: Painful muscle cramps in the legs or abdomen after physical activity in heat. Skin is moist. Person is alert.

Stage 2 — Heat Exhaustion: The body is struggling to cool itself. Symptoms: heavy sweating, cool and pale or flushed skin, fast and weak pulse, nausea or vomiting, headache, dizziness, fainting, muscle cramps, extreme tiredness. The person remains conscious and coherent.

Stage 3 — Heatstroke (Emergency): The cooling system has failed. Symptoms: body temperature above 40°C (104°F), hot and dry skin (sweating stops), rapid and strong pulse, confusion, slurred speech, agitation, seizures, loss of consciousness.

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Call for emergency help immediately for heatstroke (Stage 3):
- Hot and dry skin (no sweating despite extreme heat)
- Confusion, agitation, or loss of consciousness
- Temperature above 40°C
- Seizures
Heatstroke can cause brain damage and death within minutes — do not wait. Call Rescue 1122 or Edhi 115.

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:

For heatstroke (Stage 3) — begin aggressive cooling immediately while calling for help:
1. Move the person to shade or a cool indoor area.
2. Remove excess clothing.
3. Pour cool (not ice cold) water over the entire body, especially the neck, armpits, and groin where large blood vessels run close to the skin.
4. Fan the person vigorously while their skin is wet — this dramatically accelerates heat loss.
5. Apply wet cloths or cool damp towels to the neck, armpits, groin, and wrists.
6. If ice is available, wrap in cloth and apply to armpits, groin, and neck — do not apply ice directly to skin.
7. If the person is conscious and not vomiting: give cool water or ORS to drink.
8. If confused, vomiting, or unconscious: do not give anything by mouth.
9. If unconscious: place in recovery position and monitor breathing. Begin CPR if they stop breathing.
10. Continue cooling until the person becomes more alert or emergency help arrives.

For heat exhaustion (Stage 2):
1. Move to shade or a cool area immediately.
2. Lie the person down and elevate their legs slightly.
3. Remove excess clothing and apply cool wet cloths to face, neck, and wrists.
4. Give ORS or cool water to drink slowly — about 150–200 ml every 15 minutes.
5. If not improved in 30 minutes, or if they become confused, treat as heatstroke and call for emergency help.

For heat cramps (Stage 1):
1. Move to shade and rest.
2. Give ORS or water with a pinch of salt.

DO NOT:
- Do not give aspirin or paracetamol for heatstroke — these medications work against infection-caused fever, not heatstroke.
- Do not give fluids to an unconscious or confused person — they may inhale them.
- Do not leave a heatstroke patient alone.

HIGH-RISK GROUPS:
Prioritise monitoring for heatstroke in: elderly individuals, children under 5, pregnant women, people with heart disease or diabetes, and anyone performing heavy physical labour in heat. Never leave a child in a closed vehicle, tent, or enclosed space in heat.

PREVENTION:
- Drink water regularly — do not wait to feel thirsty. Adults need at least 2–3 litres per day in extreme heat.
- Avoid physical activity during the hottest part of the day (11:00–15:00).
- Wear light, loose, light-coloured clothing.
- Set up shade structures in relief camps as a priority for children, elderly, and sick individuals.
