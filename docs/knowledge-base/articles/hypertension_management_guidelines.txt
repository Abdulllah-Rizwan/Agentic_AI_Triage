Hypertension and Hypertensive Emergency — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: hypertension, high blood pressure, blood pressure emergency, stroke, heart attack, BP crisis

RECOGNIZING A HYPERTENSIVE EMERGENCY:
A sudden severe rise in blood pressure (typically 180/120 mmHg or higher) combined with organ damage. In disaster settings, patients with existing high blood pressure often run out of medications, face extreme stress, and cannot reach clinics — raising the risk significantly.

Seek emergency care immediately if a person with known or suspected high blood pressure develops any of:
- Severe headache that comes on suddenly — often described as the worst headache of their life
- Chest pain or tightness
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
- Blurred vision, double vision, or sudden loss of vision
- Confusion, difficulty speaking, or sudden weakness or numbness in the face, arm, or leg (stroke signs)
- Nausea and vomiting with a severe headache
- Nosebleeds that are severe and difficult to control
- Seizures in a person with no prior history
- Irregular heartbeat or pounding sensation in the chest

FAST test for stroke: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call for help immediately.

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Call Rescue 1122 or transport to hospital immediately for any hypertensive emergency. Every minute without treatment during a stroke causes further brain damage. Heart attack warning signs: crushing or squeezing chest pain spreading to the left arm, jaw, or back; sweating; nausea; shortness of breath.

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:
1. Keep the person calm and seated or lying down. Stress and physical exertion worsen blood pressure.
2. If the person has their prescribed blood pressure medication available and is conscious, encourage them to take it as directed.
3. Loosen tight clothing around the chest and neck.
4. If the person loses consciousness, check for breathing and begin CPR if they are not breathing.
5. Monitor and reassure the person while waiting for help. Do not leave them alone.

DO NOT:
- Do not give medications that have not been prescribed for them.
- Do not give aspirin unless specifically prescribed — aspirin treats heart attacks but is not appropriate for all hypertensive events.
- Do not allow the person to exert themselves physically.
- Do not leave the person alone.

MANAGING BLOOD PRESSURE IN A DISASTER SETTING:
Patients who take regular blood pressure medication must not stop suddenly — sudden discontinuation can trigger a rebound hypertensive crisis.
If medications are lost or unavailable:
- Reduce physical exertion and stay calm.
- Reduce salt intake.
- Drink adequate water.
- Notify relief medical teams immediately so medication supplies can be sourced.
Pakistan's NDMA and provincial disaster health teams deploy mobile medical units during major disasters — inform them of any patient requiring regular medication for hypertension, diabetes, or epilepsy.
