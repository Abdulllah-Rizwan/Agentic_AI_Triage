Leptospirosis — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: leptospirosis, flood fever, rat fever, weil disease, leptospira

RECOGNIZING LEPTOSPIROSIS:
A bacterial infection spread through contact with floodwater, soil, or surfaces contaminated by the urine of infected animals (rats, cattle, dogs). Very common after floods. Enters the body through cuts, open wounds, eyes, or mouth.
Symptoms appear 1–2 weeks after exposure to contaminated water:
- Sudden high fever and severe chills
- Intense muscle pain, especially in the calves and lower back
- Severe headache
- Red eyes (conjunctivitis)
- Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea
- Rash on the shins

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Go to hospital immediately if any of the following appear:
- Jaundice: yellow skin and yellow eyes (Weil's disease)
- Dark urine or reduced urine output
- Difficulty breathing or chest pain
- Confusion or neck stiffness (signs of meningitis)
- Bleeding from skin or gums
- Cannot keep any fluids down
Severe cases can lead to kidney failure, liver failure, and death without hospital care.

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:
1. Rest completely.
2. Drink plenty of clean, boiled water or ORS to prevent dehydration.
3. Take paracetamol for fever and muscle pain.
4. Do NOT take ibuprofen or aspirin — they can worsen kidney function.
5. Go to a health worker for antibiotic prescription — doxycycline or amoxicillin is the first-line treatment.

DO NOT:
- Do not take ibuprofen or aspirin — they damage kidneys which are already stressed by leptospirosis.
- Do not assume it is just a cold or flu — leptospirosis can worsen rapidly within 24–48 hours.

PREVENTION:
- Do NOT walk barefoot or wade through floodwater — wear rubber boots or waterproof footwear.
- Cover all cuts, wounds, and open sores with waterproof bandaging before any water contact.
- Do not let floodwater enter your mouth or eyes.
- Do not swim or bathe in rivers, streams, or floodwater.
- Boil all drinking water — do not drink from taps fed by flooded sources.
- Wash hands thoroughly with soap and clean water after any contact with floodwater, animals, or soil.
- Keep rats away from food storage — use covered containers and remove rubbish piles.

HIGH-RISK SITUATIONS:
- Any person who has waded through floodwater with bare legs or uncovered wounds
- Anyone exposed to rat-infested areas
- Farm workers handling cattle, pigs, or horses without protective gear
If you have been exposed and develop fever within 2 weeks — go to a health worker and mention the flood exposure.
