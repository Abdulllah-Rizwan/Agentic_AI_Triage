Crush Injury and Traumatic Entrapment — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: crush injury, trapped, building collapse, debris, entrapment, crush syndrome

RECOGNIZING CRUSH INJURY AND CRUSH SYNDROME:
Crush injuries occur when a body part is compressed under heavy weight — building collapse, floods undermining structures, landslides.

Crush syndrome (the hidden danger): When muscle is crushed for more than one hour, toxic substances are released into the bloodstream when pressure is released. This causes:
- Sudden kidney failure (dark brown or tea-coloured urine is a warning sign)
- Dangerously high potassium causing cardiac arrest
- Shock and collapse — sometimes hours after rescue when the victim seemed stable
A person who was trapped and then freed may appear alert but can collapse and die within hours if not hospitalized.

Signs of shock after crush injury:
- Pale, cold, clammy skin
- Rapid and weak pulse
- Rapid shallow breathing
- Confusion or drowsiness
- Loss of consciousness

Compartment syndrome: severe pain out of proportion to the injury, tight or shiny skin, numbness or tingling, pale or bluish skin on the affected limb — surgical emergency.

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
All crush injury victims — regardless of how stable they appear — must reach hospital. Blood tests, IV fluids, and urine monitoring are required to prevent kidney failure and cardiac arrest. Emergency contacts: Rescue 1122 (Punjab), Edhi Foundation 115, NDMA 1700.

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:

Before freeing a trapped victim:
1. Alert emergency services before freeing them if possible — IV fluids should ideally be started before release.
2. Establish voice contact with the trapped person — reassure them.
3. Do not give water through compressed soft tissue.

After freeing the victim:
1. Lay the person flat. Do not allow them to stand or walk — movement accelerates toxin release.
2. Check for and control any external bleeding with direct pressure.
3. Immobilize injured limbs — do not attempt to straighten broken bones.
4. Monitor breathing. Begin CPR immediately if they stop breathing.
5. Watch for shock signs: pale cold skin, rapid weak pulse, confusion.

For shock:
1. Lay patient flat and raise legs slightly (unless head or spinal injury suspected).
2. Keep warm.
3. Do NOT give food or water.
4. Get to hospital immediately.

For spinal injury (building collapse or fall):
1. Assume spinal injury is possible.
2. Do not bend, twist, or move the neck or spine.
3. If they must be moved: keep the head, neck, and back in a straight line — one person holds the head while others move the body as a unit.
4. Place on a rigid flat surface (wooden board, door) if available.

For fractures from crush injury:
1. Splint the limb in the position found using a padded rigid board.
2. For leg fractures: tie injured leg to uninjured leg with cloth strips.
3. For arm fractures: tie arm to padded board and support in a sling.
4. Do not try to realign a bone. Cover open fractures with a clean damp cloth.

DO NOT:
- Do not allow a freed crush victim to walk or stand — toxin release accelerates with movement.
- Do not assume the patient is stable because they are alert — collapse can happen hours later.
- Do not bend or twist the spine when moving a victim from a collapsed building.
