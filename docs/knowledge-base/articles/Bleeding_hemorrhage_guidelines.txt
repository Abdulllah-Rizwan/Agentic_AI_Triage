Bleeding and Hemorrhage — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: bleeding, hemorrhage, khoon, severe bleeding, uncontrolled bleeding, cut artery

RECOGNIZING SEVERE BLEEDING:
- Blood soaking through clothing or dressings rapidly
- Blood is bright red and spurting in pulses (arterial bleed — most dangerous)
- Wound to the chest, abdomen, neck, or groin
- Large wound with gaping edges
- Embedded object (knife, glass, metal) in the wound
- Signs of internal bleeding: bruising, swelling, rigid abdomen, pale and cold skin, rapid weak pulse, patient feels faint or confused (no visible wound)

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Go to hospital immediately if:
- Bleeding does not slow after 10 minutes of firm pressure
- Suspected internal bleeding
- Large wound requiring sutures
- Blood is bright red and spurting (arterial bleed)
- Patient is losing consciousness, pale, or confused
- Penetrating wound to chest, abdomen, neck, or groin
- Embedded object in wound

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:

For severe external bleeding:
1. Apply direct pressure immediately. Use a clean cloth, bandage, or clothing. Press firmly on the wound without lifting or checking every few seconds — continuous pressure is what stops bleeding. Hold for at least 10 minutes without releasing.
2. If blood soaks through, do NOT remove the cloth. Add more material on top and press harder.
3. Elevate the injured limb above heart level if possible while maintaining pressure.

For limb bleeding that will not stop with pressure — tourniquet:
1. Apply 5–7 cm (2–3 inches) above the wound, not on a joint.
2. Tighten until bleeding stops completely — it must be tight enough to hurt.
3. Write the time of application on the person's skin or forehead (e.g. "TK 14:30").
4. Do NOT remove the tourniquet once applied — only medical staff should remove it.
5. A tourniquet can stay on safely for up to 2 hours.

For embedded objects (knife, glass, debris):
1. Do NOT remove the object — it may be plugging a vessel.
2. Pad around the object to keep it stable and apply pressure around the sides.
3. Transport immediately to hospital.

For internal bleeding (no visible wound):
1. Lay patient flat, raise legs 30 cm.
2. Keep warm.
3. Do NOT give food or water.
4. Get to hospital immediately — internal bleeding cannot be treated in the field.

For nosebleed:
1. Sit the patient upright and tilt head slightly forward — NOT backward.
2. Pinch the soft part of the nose firmly for 10–15 minutes without releasing.
3. Breathe through the mouth.
4. If bleeding continues beyond 20 minutes or follows a head injury — seek emergency care.

DO NOT:
- Do not remove embedded objects from wounds.
- Do not check a pressure dressing every few minutes — this breaks the clot forming underneath.
- Do not apply a tourniquet on a joint.
- Do not remove a tourniquet once applied in the field.
