Bleeding and Hemorrhage — Field First Aid

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS FOR SEVERE BLEEDING

Apply direct pressure immediately. Use a clean cloth, bandage, or clothing. Press firmly on the wound without lifting or checking every few seconds — continuous pressure is what stops bleeding. Hold for at least 10 minutes without releasing.

If blood soaks through, do NOT remove the cloth. Add more material on top and press harder.

Elevate the injured limb above heart level if possible while maintaining pressure.

FOR LIMB BLEEDING THAT WILL NOT STOP WITH PRESSURE — TOURNIQUET:
1. Apply 5–7 cm (2–3 inches) above the wound, not on a joint.
2. Tighten until bleeding stops completely — it must be tight enough to hurt.
3. Write the time of application on the person's skin or forehead (e.g. "TK 14:30").
4. Do NOT remove the tourniquet once applied — only medical staff should remove it.
5. A tourniquet can stay on safely for up to 2 hours.

FOR EMBEDDED OBJECTS (knife, glass, debris):
- Do NOT remove the object — it may be plugging a vessel.
- Pad around the object to keep it stable and apply pressure around the sides.
- Transport immediately to hospital.

FOR INTERNAL BLEEDING (no visible wound):
Signs: bruising, swelling, rigid abdomen, pale and cold skin, rapid weak pulse, patient feels faint or confused.
Action: Lay patient flat, raise legs 30 cm, keep warm, do NOT give food or water, get to hospital immediately. Internal bleeding cannot be treated in the field.

NOSEBLEED:
1. Sit the patient upright and tilt head slightly forward (NOT backward — blood goes to stomach).
2. Pinch the soft part of the nose firmly for 10–15 minutes without releasing.
3. Breathe through the mouth.
4. Apply a cold wet cloth to the forehead and back of neck.
5. If bleeding continues beyond 20 minutes or follows a head injury — seek emergency care.

WOUND PACKING (for deep wounds where surface pressure is insufficient):
1. Pack clean cloth or gauze firmly into the wound cavity.
2. Apply hard pressure over the packed wound.
3. Maintain pressure and transport to hospital.

SIGNS THAT REQUIRE IMMEDIATE HOSPITAL TRANSPORT:
- Bleeding does not slow after 10 minutes of firm pressure
- Suspected internal bleeding
- Large wound requiring sutures
- Blood is bright red and spurting (arterial bleed)
- Patient is losing consciousness, pale, or confused
- Penetrating wound to chest, abdomen, neck, or groin
