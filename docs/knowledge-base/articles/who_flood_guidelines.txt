Flood Health and Safety — WHO Guide

TOPIC: flood, flooding, floodwater, baarish, selaab, flood safety, flood disease

RECOGNIZING FLOOD-RELATED HEALTH RISKS:
Health hazards during and after floods:
- Waterborne diseases: cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A, and severe diarrhea from drinking or contact with contaminated floodwater.
- Vector-borne diseases: malaria and dengue spread through mosquitoes that breed in standing floodwater.
- Rodent-borne disease: leptospirosis spread through contact with water contaminated by rat urine.
- Snake bites: snakes are displaced from their habitat and enter homes and shelters during floods.
- Hypothermia: prolonged exposure to cold floodwater causes dangerous drop in body temperature.
- Electrocution: submerged wiring, downed power lines, and flooded appliances.
- Injuries: falls on wet unstable ground, debris, hidden holes under murky water.

Signs of hypothermia (cold exposure):
- Shivering, pale and cold skin, lips turning blue
- Slurred speech, confusion, slow breathing, weak pulse
- In babies: unusually quiet, refusing to feed, red skin that is cold to the touch

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Seek emergency care immediately if:
- High fever with severe headache, muscle pain, or red eyes after flood exposure — possible leptospirosis
- Severe diarrhea or vomiting — risk of rapid dehydration
- Difficulty breathing or chest pain
- Unconsciousness or extreme confusion
- Snake or animal bite
- Signs of heatstroke: confusion, hot dry skin
- Wounds or skin cuts that appear infected after flood exposure
- Jaundice (yellow skin or eyes) after flood contact
- Signs of electrocution

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:

For hypothermia (cold exposure from floodwater):
1. Move the person to a warm, dry, sheltered place immediately.
2. Remove all wet clothing and wrap in dry blankets or towels, covering the head.
3. Give warm (not hot), non-alcoholic, caffeine-free drinks if the person is fully awake and can swallow.
4. Give sugary food such as biscuits or chocolate if conscious.
5. Keep the person awake by talking to them. Do not leave them alone.
6. Do not rub limbs vigorously — rewarm gently. Do not apply direct heat (hot water bottles on bare skin).

For waterborne illness (diarrhea, vomiting, fever):
1. Give ORS immediately — dissolve 1 ORS packet in 1 litre of clean boiled water, or make at home: 1 litre water + 6 teaspoons sugar + 1/2 teaspoon salt.
2. Continue giving fluids until diarrhea stops.
3. Give paracetamol for fever.
4. Seek medical care — antibiotics may be needed.

For all flood survivors:
1. Boil all water before drinking, brushing teeth, or cleaning wounds.
2. Wash hands with soap for at least 40 seconds before eating and after toilet use.
3. Cover all cuts and wounds with waterproof dressings before any contact with floodwater.

DO NOT:
- Do not walk or drive through floodwater — 15 cm of fast-moving water can knock an adult over; 30 cm can sweep away a vehicle.
- Do not enter a flooded building without checking for structural damage.
- Do not turn on electrical appliances in a flooded building — have wiring checked by an electrician first.
- Do not approach downed power lines — assume they are live.
- Do not eat food that has been in contact with floodwater.
- Do not throw away food after a power cut until you check it — after 4 hours without power, refrigerated meats, fish, and cooked food should be discarded.
- Do not share unverified health information — follow official guidance from NDMA, WHO, and provincial disaster authorities.

PREVENTION:
- Before floods: prepare an emergency kit with ORS packets, regular medications, clean water, first aid supplies, and important documents in a waterproof bag.
- Store food in waterproof sealed containers above floor level.
- Seal all entrances to your home against rodents — floodwaters displace rats which carry leptospirosis.
- Use mosquito repellent and sleep under nets — standing floodwater breeds malaria and dengue mosquitoes.
- Wear rubber boots when in flooded areas to prevent leptospirosis entry through cuts and skin.
- Keep emergency contacts saved: Rescue 1122 (Punjab), Edhi Foundation 115, NDMA Helpline 1700.
