Protecting Your Health Before, During and After Floods — WHO Guide

Floods are among the most frequent and deadly natural disasters. In Pakistan, monsoon floods affect millions of people each year, displacing families and creating serious health risks. This guide covers what to do before, during, and after a flood to protect your health.

BEFORE A FLOOD — PREPARE

Prepare an emergency kit with: medicines you take regularly, oral rehydration salts (ORS), clean drinking water, first aid supplies, and important documents in a waterproof bag.
Identify your nearest emergency assembly point and shelter.
Notify local authorities if you or a family member has a disability or chronic illness that requires extra support during evacuation.
Store food in waterproof, sealed containers above floor level.
Keep emergency contact numbers saved: Rescue 1122 (Punjab), Edhi Foundation 115, NDMA Helpline 1700.

DURING A FLOOD — STAY SAFE

Do not walk or drive through floodwater. Even 15 cm of fast-moving water can knock an adult off their feet. 30 cm can sweep away a vehicle.
If trapped on a roof, tree, or elevated structure, stay there and wait for rescue — do not enter the water.
Floodwater is dangerous: it may contain raw sewage, chemicals, sharp objects, and submerged hazards such as open manholes and downed power lines.
Do not touch electrical equipment if you are wet or standing in water. If safe to do so, turn off electricity at the mains.
Only evacuate if instructed by authorities. Follow official guidance.

AFTER A FLOOD — PROTECT HEALTH

Only return home when officially declared safe to do so. Check for structural damage before entering any building.

HYPOTHERMIA (COLD EXPOSURE)

Staying in water below 20°C for extended periods causes hypothermia — dangerous drop in body temperature below 35°C.

Signs of hypothermia: shivering, pale and cold skin, lips turning blue, slurred speech, confusion, slow breathing, and weak pulse. A baby with hypothermia may be unusually quiet, refuse to feed, and have red skin that is cold to touch.

First aid for hypothermia:
Move the person indoors to a warm, dry place immediately.
Remove wet clothing and wrap them in a blanket or dry towels, covering the head.
Give warm, non-alcoholic, caffeine-free drinks if fully awake.
Give sugary food such as chocolate or biscuits if conscious.
Keep the person awake by talking to them. Stay with them until help arrives.
Do not rub the limbs vigorously — rewarm gently.

WATERBORNE DISEASE PREVENTION

Floods contaminate drinking water with sewage and bacteria, causing cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A, and severe diarrhoea.

Boil all water before drinking, brushing teeth, cleaning wounds, or preparing food. Bring water to a rolling boil, remove from heat, and let it cool in a covered clean container.
Use bottled water with unbroken seals if available.
Wash hands with soap and water for 40–60 seconds before eating and after using the toilet.
Use water purification tablets if boiling is not possible.

FOOD SAFETY AFTER FLOODS

Do not eat food that has been in contact with floodwater.
Throw away all food from your fridge and freezer if power was off for more than 4 hours.
Do not eat food that smells unusual or shows signs of spoilage.
If in doubt, throw it out.
Eat perishable foods first if they appear uncontaminated. Rely on tinned or pre-packaged food otherwise.

DISEASES SPREAD BY RODENTS AND MOSQUITOES

Floodwater displaces rats and other rodents, which carry leptospirosis and other infections.
Seal all entrances to your home against rodents.
Wear rubber boots and waterproof gloves in flooded areas.
Cover any cuts or skin wounds with waterproof dressings before contact with floodwater.
Standing water breeds mosquitoes. Use mosquito repellent, sleep under nets, and wear long sleeves after dark.

SNAKE BITES DURING FLOODS

Snakes are displaced by floods and may enter homes and shelters.
If bitten, seek emergency hospital care immediately — antivenom is the only effective treatment.
Keep the victim still and immobilize the bitten limb.
Do not cut the wound, suck the venom, or apply a tourniquet.

WHEN TO SEEK MEDICAL HELP IMMEDIATELY

Seek emergency care at the nearest functioning hospital if you experience:
Injury or a wound that may be contaminated by floodwater.
High fever, strong headache, severe diarrhoea, or heavy vomiting.
Difficulty breathing or chest pain.
Symptoms after contact with floodwater: yellow skin or eyes (jaundice), muscle pain, red eyes — these may indicate leptospirosis or hepatitis.
Animal or snake bite.
Signs of heatstroke: confusion, hot dry skin, not sweating.
Mental distress: persistent distressing memories of the flood, inability to function, aggressive behaviour — seek psychological first aid or mental health support.

If you need to access a hospital during a flood emergency: check official information about which hospitals are functioning; let health workers know about any chronic conditions or regular medications you take; understand that the most critically injured will be seen first.

STAYING INFORMED

During emergencies, false information spreads rapidly. Only follow guidance from official sources: Pakistan NDMA (ndma.gov.pk), provincial disaster authorities, and WHO. Do not share unverified health information.
