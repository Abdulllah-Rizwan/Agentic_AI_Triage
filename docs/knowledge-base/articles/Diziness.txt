Dizziness and Vertigo — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: dizziness, vertigo, lightheadedness, feeling faint, chakkar

RECOGNIZING DIZZINESS AND ITS CAUSES:
Dizziness is a feeling that you or the room is spinning, or a sensation of being about to faint. Common causes in disaster settings:
- Dehydration: most common cause after heat, diarrhea, or inadequate fluid intake. Associated with dry mouth, dark urine, and fatigue.
- Heat exhaustion: dizziness with heavy sweating, pale skin, nausea, and rapid heartbeat.
- Low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia): dizziness with shakiness, sweating, hunger, and confusion — common if meals were missed.
- Standing up too quickly (postural hypotension): brief dizziness when rising from sitting or lying. Usually passes in seconds.
- Vertigo (BPPV): a sudden strong spinning sensation triggered by head movement. Room feels like it is rotating.
- Anxiety and hyperventilation: dizziness with fast breathing, chest tightness, and tingling in the hands.
- Medication side effects: dizziness as a side effect of blood pressure tablets, antibiotics, or other drugs.

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Seek emergency help immediately if dizziness is accompanied by:
- Sudden severe headache — the worst headache of your life (possible stroke or bleed)
- Weakness or numbness on one side of the face, arm, or leg
- Slurred speech, confusion, or difficulty understanding words
- Double vision or sudden loss of vision
- Loss of consciousness or fainting
- Chest pain or palpitations
- Vomiting that will not stop
- Dizziness after a head injury
- A person over 60 with new dizziness and any of the above — risk of stroke is higher
Use the FAST test: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call for help — any one of these with dizziness is a stroke emergency.

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:
1. Sit or lie down immediately — do not try to walk while dizzy. Most falls happen when dizzy patients try to continue standing.
2. If lying down: keep the head and upper body slightly elevated (not completely flat).
3. Stay still until the dizziness passes — sudden movement makes vertigo worse.
4. If dehydration is suspected: drink small frequent sips of water or ORS. Do not gulp large amounts at once.
5. If low blood sugar is suspected (shaky, sweaty, hungry): give sugar — a sweet drink, biscuit, or a spoonful of sugar. If the person improves within 10–15 minutes, this confirms hypoglycaemia. Seek medical care for the underlying cause.
6. For heat-related dizziness: move to shade or a cool place, loosen clothing, apply cool wet cloth to the neck and forehead.
7. When rising after dizziness passes: move slowly — sit up first, wait 30 seconds, then stand. Hold onto something for support.
8. If dizziness is recurrent or does not improve after rest and fluids, see a health worker.

DO NOT:
- Do not drive, climb, or operate any machinery while dizzy.
- Do not bend down suddenly or get up from lying position quickly.
- Do not give dizziness patients coffee, alcohol, or carbonated drinks — these worsen dehydration.
- Do not leave an elderly person or child alone while they are dizzy — fall risk is high.

PREVENTION:
- Drink adequate water throughout the day — at least 2 litres in normal conditions, more in heat.
- Do not skip meals, especially if taking medications that can lower blood sugar.
- Rise slowly from sitting or lying positions, especially in the morning or after long periods of rest.
- In hot weather, move to shade regularly and drink ORS if sweating heavily.
