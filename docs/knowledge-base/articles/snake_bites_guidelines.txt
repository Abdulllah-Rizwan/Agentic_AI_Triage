Snake Bites: Emergency First Aid — Pakistan Context

Snake bites are a serious medical emergency, especially during and after floods when snakes are displaced from their habitats into populated areas. In Pakistan, venomous snakes responsible for most serious bites include Russell's viper (Daboia russelii), saw-scaled viper (Echis carinatus), common krait (Bungarus caeruleus), and Indian cobra (Naja naja).

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS IF BITTEN

Call for emergency help immediately. In Pakistan: Rescue 1122 (Punjab), Edhi Foundation 115, or take the patient to the nearest hospital with antivenom stock.

Keep the victim calm and as still as possible. Movement speeds venom spread through the lymphatic system.

Immobilize the bitten limb using a splint or sling. Keep it at or below heart level.

Remove rings, bracelets, watches, and tight clothing near the bite site — swelling will occur rapidly.

Note the time of the bite and, if possible, describe the snake's color and pattern to medical staff. Do not attempt to catch or kill the snake.

WHAT NOT TO DO

Do not cut the wound or attempt to suck out venom — this does not work and causes infection.
Do not apply a tourniquet — this traps venom and causes tissue death.
Do not apply ice, cold compresses, or herbal remedies to the bite.
Do not give the victim alcohol.
Do not raise the bitten limb above the level of the heart.
Do not rub or massage the wound area.

RECOGNIZING A VENOMOUS BITE

Signs that a snake bite is venomous include: rapid swelling and bruising around the bite, severe local pain, bleeding from the bite site, bleeding from gums or other body openings, drooping eyelids, difficulty breathing, blurred vision, numbness or tingling in the face and limbs, nausea and vomiting, and sudden drop in consciousness.

Russell's Viper (most common dangerous snake in Pakistan): Causes massive local swelling, bleeding from the bite and gums, kidney failure. Symptoms appear within minutes to hours.

Saw-Scaled Viper: Small snake with a rough, scaly appearance. Causes local tissue destruction and severe bleeding. Often found in sandy or rocky areas and is highly aggressive.

Common Krait: Nocturnal, often bites people while they sleep. Bite may be painless at first. Causes progressive paralysis — drooping eyelids, weakness, difficulty breathing. Symptoms can be delayed 1–6 hours.

Indian Cobra: Hood-spreading large snake. Bite causes both local swelling and systemic effects including paralysis of breathing muscles.

ANTIVENOM — THE ONLY EFFECTIVE TREATMENT

Antivenom is the only treatment that reverses venom effects. It must be administered by trained medical staff in a healthcare facility. The sooner antivenom is given after a bite, the more effective it is. Polyvalent antivenom covering Russell's viper, saw-scaled viper, cobra, and krait is available at major public hospitals in Pakistan.

Time is critical: aim to reach hospital within 1–2 hours of the bite.

DURING FLOODS: SNAKE BITE PREVENTION

During floods, snakes escape rising water and may hide in debris, clothing, blankets, and dark corners of evacuated homes.

Wear rubber boots and thick socks when walking through flooded or waterlogged areas.
Shake out bedding, shoes, and clothing before use.
Use a torch/flashlight at night — kraits are particularly active after dark.
Do not put hands into holes, debris piles, or flooded areas without checking first.
Keep children away from debris fields and stagnant water edges.
