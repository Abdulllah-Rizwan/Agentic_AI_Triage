Snake Bites — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: snake bite, saanp ka kaatna, venomous snake, viper bite, cobra bite, krait bite

RECOGNIZING A VENOMOUS SNAKE BITE:
Signs that a snake bite is venomous:
- Rapid swelling and bruising around the bite
- Severe local pain
- Bleeding from the bite site or from gums
- Drooping eyelids
- Difficulty breathing
- Blurred vision
- Numbness or tingling in the face and limbs
- Nausea and vomiting
- Sudden drop in consciousness
Common venomous snakes in Pakistan:
- Russell's Viper: massive local swelling, bleeding from bite and gums, kidney failure. Symptoms appear within minutes to hours.
- Saw-Scaled Viper: local tissue destruction and severe bleeding.
- Common Krait: nocturnal, bite may be painless at first. Progressive paralysis — drooping eyelids, weakness, difficulty breathing. Symptoms can be delayed 1–6 hours.
- Indian Cobra: local swelling and paralysis of breathing muscles.

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
All snake bites are medical emergencies. Go to the nearest hospital with antivenom stock immediately. Antivenom is the only effective treatment. Time is critical — aim to reach hospital within 1–2 hours of the bite.
Call for help: Rescue 1122 (Punjab), Edhi Foundation 115.

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:
1. Call for emergency help immediately.
2. Keep the victim calm and as still as possible — movement speeds venom spread through the lymphatic system.
3. Immobilize the bitten limb using a splint or sling. Keep it at or below heart level.
4. Remove rings, bracelets, watches, and tight clothing near the bite site — swelling will occur rapidly.
5. Note the time of the bite. If possible, describe the snake's colour and pattern to medical staff. Do not attempt to catch or kill the snake.
6. Keep the victim lying down and calm while transport is arranged.

DO NOT:
- Do not cut the wound or attempt to suck out venom — this does not work and causes infection.
- Do not apply a tourniquet — this traps venom and causes tissue death.
- Do not apply ice, cold compresses, or herbal remedies to the bite.
- Do not give the victim alcohol.
- Do not raise the bitten limb above the level of the heart.
- Do not rub or massage the wound area.

PREVENTION DURING FLOODS:
During floods, snakes escape rising water and hide in debris, clothing, blankets, and dark corners of evacuated homes.
- Wear rubber boots and thick socks when walking through flooded or waterlogged areas.
- Shake out bedding, shoes, and clothing before use.
- Use a torch/flashlight at night — kraits are particularly active after dark.
- Do not put hands into holes, debris piles, or flooded areas without checking first.
- Keep children away from debris fields and stagnant water edges.
