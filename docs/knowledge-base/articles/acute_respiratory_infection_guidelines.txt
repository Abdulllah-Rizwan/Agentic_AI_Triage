Acute Respiratory Infection — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: respiratory infection, chest infection, breathing difficulty, saans ki takleef, pneumonia, cough fever, flu

RECOGNIZING RESPIRATORY INFECTIONS:

Upper respiratory infection (common cold or flu): runny nose, sore throat, mild fever, cough. Usually self-limiting in 7–10 days.

Pneumonia (lower respiratory infection — serious):
- Cough with yellow, green, or brown phlegm (sputum)
- Fever above 38°C, often with chills
- Rapid breathing (see thresholds below)
- Chest pain that worsens with breathing or coughing
- Shortness of breath, especially at rest
- In children: lower chest wall indrawing — the lower chest pulls inward during breathing
- In severe cases: bluish lips or fingernails, confusion, inability to feed (infants), severe lethargy

BREATHING RATE — A CRITICAL SIGN:
Breathing rate is the most reliable early sign of pneumonia in children. Count breaths per minute with child calm and quiet.
- Under 2 months: more than 60 breaths/minute = fast
- 2 to 12 months: more than 50 breaths/minute = fast
- 1 to 5 years: more than 40 breaths/minute = fast
- Adults: more than 30 breaths/minute at rest = warning sign
If rapid breathing is present, seek medical care immediately.

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Take the patient to hospital without delay if any of the following are present:
- Difficulty breathing at rest or severe shortness of breath
- Lips or fingernails turning blue (cyanosis)
- Chest indrawing in children
- Inability to drink or breastfeed in infants
- Stridor (high-pitched crowing sound when inhaling)
- Altered consciousness, drowsiness, or convulsions with fever
- Breathing rate above the thresholds listed above
- Patient getting worse after 48 hours of home management

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:

For upper respiratory infections (cold, mild flu):
1. Rest and increase fluid intake — water, ORS, clear soup, breast milk for infants.
2. Give paracetamol for fever and discomfort. Do not use aspirin in children.
3. One teaspoon of honey 3 times daily can soothe cough in children over 1 year. Do not give honey to infants under 1 year.
4. Saline nasal drops (half teaspoon salt in 100 ml clean water) for nasal congestion.
5. Ensure adequate warm clothing and shelter.
6. Monitor breathing rate daily. Seek care if rate increases.

For suspected pneumonia (while getting to hospital):
1. Keep the patient sitting upright or with head elevated — lying flat worsens breathing.
2. Loosen tight clothing around the chest and neck.
3. Keep the patient warm.
4. Give paracetamol for fever.
5. If prescribed amoxicillin by a health worker, give the full 5-day course even if the patient improves.

DO NOT:
- Do not give cough syrups to children under 6 years — they are ineffective and some are harmful.
- Do not give antibiotics without medical assessment — antibiotics are ineffective against viral respiratory infections.
- Do not delay if breathing is fast or difficult — pneumonia kills children within hours.

PREVENTION IN RELIEF CAMPS:
- Ventilate shelters as much as possible — avoid overcrowded enclosed spaces.
- Avoid cooking fires inside tents — smoke worsens respiratory illness and causes pneumonia in children.
- Handwashing with soap prevents spread of respiratory viruses.
- Prioritize care for malnourished children — malnutrition severely impairs immunity against respiratory infection.
