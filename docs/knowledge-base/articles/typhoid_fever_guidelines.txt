Typhoid Fever — Recognition and Emergency Action

RECOGNISE TYPHOID — DISTINCTIVE SIGNS

Typhoid has specific signs that separate it from common stomach illnesses. Look for:
- Fever that builds slowly over several days and stays high (39–40°C / 102–104°F) for a week or more without resolving
- Slow heart rate despite a high fever (pulse is lower than expected — this is a key distinguishing feature)
- Rose-coloured flat spots on the chest or abdomen (appear in some patients in the second week)
- Severe fatigue and weakness out of proportion to other symptoms
- Abdominal tenderness and bloating
- The fever came on gradually over days, NOT suddenly

Unlike a typical stomach bug, typhoid fever does not resolve in 1–2 days — it worsens progressively if untreated.

EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS — GO TO HOSPITAL IMMEDIATELY

Call for help or go to the nearest hospital without delay if any of the following appear:
- Sudden severe abdominal pain or a rigid, board-hard abdomen — indicates possible intestinal perforation (surgical emergency)
- Blood in stools or vomiting blood
- High fever with confusion, delirium, or unconsciousness
- Seizures or convulsions
- Complete inability to take any fluids — risk of collapse

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS BEFORE REACHING HOSPITAL

- Keep the patient lying flat and resting — no physical exertion
- Give oral rehydration salts (ORS) or clean boiled water in small, frequent sips if the patient is conscious and can swallow
- Give paracetamol to reduce fever — do NOT give aspirin or ibuprofen (increases bleeding risk)
- Do NOT give any medicine to stop loose stools — this traps the bacteria inside the gut

PREVENTION IN DISASTER-AFFECTED AREAS

- Drink only boiled water or sealed bottled water — never untreated flood water
- Wash hands with soap before eating and after toilet use
- Avoid raw vegetables, unpeeled fruit, and street food prepared with unclean water
- Avoid ice in drinks unless made from safe water
- Typhoid vaccination is recommended for people in flood-affected or endemic areas
- Report any cluster of prolonged fever cases to the nearest medical relief team

TYPHOID IN CHILDREN

Children under 5 are at highest risk. A child with a high fever lasting more than 3 days without an obvious cause (such as a visible wound or cold) should be taken for medical assessment. Do not wait for other symptoms to appear — seek care early.
