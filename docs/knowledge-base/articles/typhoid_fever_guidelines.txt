Typhoid Fever — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: typhoid, typhoid fever, enteric fever, tifo, persistent fever, typhoid bukhar

RECOGNIZING TYPHOID — DISTINCTIVE SIGNS:
Typhoid has specific signs that separate it from common stomach illnesses. Look for:
- Fever that builds slowly over several days and stays high (39–40°C / 102–104°F) for a week or more without resolving
- Slow heart rate despite a high fever (pulse is lower than expected — a key distinguishing feature)
- Rose-coloured flat spots on the chest or abdomen (appear in some patients in the second week)
- Severe fatigue and weakness out of proportion to other symptoms
- Abdominal tenderness and bloating
- The fever came on gradually over days, NOT suddenly
Unlike a typical stomach bug, typhoid fever does not resolve in 1–2 days — it worsens progressively if untreated.

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Go to hospital without delay if any of the following appear:
- Sudden severe abdominal pain or a rigid, board-hard abdomen — possible intestinal perforation (surgical emergency)
- Blood in stools or vomiting blood
- High fever with confusion, delirium, or unconsciousness
- Seizures or convulsions
- Complete inability to take any fluids — risk of collapse

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:
1. Keep the patient lying flat and resting — no physical exertion.
2. Give oral rehydration salts (ORS) or clean boiled water in small, frequent sips if the patient is conscious and can swallow.
3. Give paracetamol to reduce fever — do NOT give aspirin or ibuprofen (increases bleeding risk).
4. Do NOT give any medicine to stop loose stools — this traps the bacteria inside the gut.
5. Monitor for emergency warning signs above — if any appear, go to hospital immediately.

DO NOT:
- Do not give aspirin or ibuprofen — they increase the risk of intestinal bleeding in typhoid.
- Do not give anti-diarrhoeal medication — it traps bacteria in the gut.
- Do not allow the patient to eat solid food if abdominal pain is present.

PREVENTION:
- Drink only boiled water or sealed bottled water — never untreated flood water.
- Wash hands with soap before eating and after toilet use.
- Avoid raw vegetables, unpeeled fruit, and street food prepared with unclean water.
- Avoid ice in drinks unless made from safe water.
- Typhoid vaccination is recommended for people in flood-affected or endemic areas.
- Report any cluster of prolonged fever cases to the nearest medical relief team.

TYPHOID IN CHILDREN:
Children under 5 are at highest risk. A child with a high fever lasting more than 3 days without an obvious cause should be taken for medical assessment. Do not wait for other symptoms to appear.
