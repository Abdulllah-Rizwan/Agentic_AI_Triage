Malnutrition in Disaster Settings — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: malnutrition, severe malnutrition, malnourished child, wasting, kwashiorkor, food emergency, hungry child

RECOGNIZING SEVERE ACUTE MALNUTRITION IN CHILDREN:
Measure the mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) using a tape or a paper strip marked at 11.5 cm and 12.5 cm:
- Red zone (below 11.5 cm): Severe Acute Malnutrition — urgent therapeutic feeding and medical care required.
- Yellow zone (11.5–12.5 cm): Moderate Acute Malnutrition — supplementary feeding required.
- Green zone (above 12.5 cm): Normal — monitor and ensure adequate diet.

How to measure: bend child's left arm at 90 degrees, find midpoint between shoulder and elbow, straighten the arm and measure around the midpoint with the tape snug but not squeezing.

Bilateral pitting oedema (always an emergency): press both feet firmly with thumbs for 3 seconds. If a pit (dent) remains in both feet — this is oedema, a sign of Severe Acute Malnutrition regardless of MUAC.

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Seek emergency hospital care immediately for a malnourished child with any of:
- High fever or hypothermia (belly is cold to touch)
- Severe dehydration
- Breathlessness or rapid breathing
- Loss of consciousness or extreme lethargy
- Inability to drink or feed
- Seizures
- Bilateral pitting oedema

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:
If Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF — brand names: Plumpy'Nut, Wawa Mum) is available:
1. Give 3–4 sachets per day spread across meals.
2. Continue breastfeeding alongside RUTF.
3. Do not share RUTF with other family members — it is medicine, not food.
4. Bring the child to a health worker weekly for weight monitoring.
5. Do not give RUTF to children who are too sick to swallow — they require IV treatment first.

For moderate malnutrition without RUTF:
1. Give supplementary food provided by relief agencies.
2. Increase feeding frequency — malnourished children need 6–8 small meals per day.
3. Continue breastfeeding for children under 2 years.
4. Energy-dense, locally available foods: khichri (rice and dal), chapati with ghee, milk, eggs, fish, lentils, green leafy vegetables.
5. Treat any infection promptly — infections worsen malnutrition.

For pregnant and breastfeeding women:
1. Ensure iron-folic acid supplements daily throughout pregnancy.
2. Adequate caloric intake — at least 300 additional calories per day above normal requirements.
3. Report to camp health coordinator — their needs must be prioritised.

DO NOT:
- Do not give RUTF to a child too sick or weak to swallow safely — aspiration risk.
- Do not share therapeutic food intended for the malnourished child with other family members.
- Do not stop treating infections in a malnourished child — infections and malnutrition worsen each other.

PREVENTION:
- In relief camps, community kitchens should prioritize supplementary feeding for: children under 5, pregnant and breastfeeding women, and elderly individuals.
- Vitamin A supplementation campaigns should be run by health authorities during flood responses.
- Report all pregnant women in displaced communities to the camp health coordinator.
