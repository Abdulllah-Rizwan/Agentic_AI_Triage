Seizure (Convulsion) — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: seizure, convulsion, epilepsy, murghi, mirgi, fit, febrile convulsion, shaking

RECOGNIZING A SEIZURE:
Convulsive seizure (most recognisable): sudden loss of consciousness, body goes rigid, then rhythmic jerking movements of arms and legs. May last 1–3 minutes. The person may cry out at the start, bite their tongue, lose bladder or bowel control, and be confused and drowsy afterward.

Absence seizure: brief staring episode, the person appears to switch off for seconds. More common in children.

Partial seizure: one limb or one side of the body twitches while the person remains conscious.

Febrile convulsion (in children): seizure triggered by a rapidly rising high fever. Very common in Pakistani children under 5. Usually brief but requires medical evaluation.

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Call for emergency help immediately if:
- The seizure lasts more than 5 minutes (status epilepticus — can cause brain damage)
- The person does not regain consciousness within 10 minutes after the seizure stops
- A second seizure follows shortly after the first
- The person has difficulty breathing or lips/skin turn blue after the seizure
- The person is injured during the seizure
- The seizure occurs in water (drowning risk)
- The person is pregnant
- This is the person's first-ever seizure
- The person has diabetes — seizures can indicate dangerously low blood sugar

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:

During the seizure:
1. Protect from injury — clear away hard or sharp objects. Place something soft under their head.
2. Turn gently onto their side (recovery position) to prevent choking if they vomit.
3. Do not restrain the person — let the seizure run its course.
4. Loosen any tight clothing around the neck.
5. Stay with the person and stay calm. Time the seizure from the start.

After the seizure:
1. The person will be confused, drowsy, or upset for several minutes — this is normal (post-ictal phase). Speak calmly and reassure them.
2. Keep them in the recovery position until fully awake.
3. Check for any injuries from the fall or movements.
4. Do not give food or water until fully alert.

For febrile convulsions in children with fever:
1. Remove excess clothing to cool the child — do not place in cold water.
2. Place the child on their side after the convulsion stops.
3. Give paracetamol for fever once the child is conscious.
4. Seek medical evaluation after every febrile convulsion, even if brief.
5. If it lasts more than 5 minutes or the child does not recover normally, go to the emergency department immediately.

DO NOT:
- Do not restrain the person or hold their limbs down.
- Do not put anything in their mouth — not fingers, not a spoon, not a cloth. People cannot swallow their tongue during a seizure. Objects in the mouth cause broken teeth, jaw injuries, and bitten fingers.
- Do not give food or water until the person is fully alert.
- Do not leave the person alone until fully conscious.

EPILEPSY PATIENTS WITHOUT MEDICATION IN DISASTER SETTINGS:
Epilepsy is common in Pakistan. Patients who run out of anti-epileptic medication during disasters are at high risk of prolonged or repeated seizures. Inform relief medical teams immediately — epilepsy medication must be prioritised as a life-saving supply. NDMA mobile medical units carry essential neurological medications.
