Tetanus — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: tetanus, lockjaw, tetanus wound, rusty nail, dirty wound, jaw stiffness

RECOGNIZING TETANUS RISK AND SYMPTOMS:
High-risk wounds (tetanus thrives in these):
Any wound caused by soil, dust, mud, manure, rusty metal, animal bites, burns, or dirty objects.

Early warning signs of tetanus (appear 3–21 days after the wound — most within 14 days):
- Jaw stiffness or difficulty opening the mouth ("lockjaw")
- Neck stiffness
- Difficulty swallowing
- Muscle spasms in the back, abdomen, or limbs — triggered by noise, light, or touch
- Fever and sweating
- Headache

In newborns (neonatal tetanus):
- Inability to suck or breastfeed in first 28 days of life
- Excessive crying
- Muscle rigidity or spasms
Neonatal tetanus is a medical emergency — take to hospital immediately.

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
If any tetanus symptoms appear — go to hospital immediately. There is no home treatment for tetanus. Hospital care is required: antitoxin injection, muscle relaxants, wound debridement, airway support.

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:

For a high-risk wound (before symptoms appear):
1. Clean the wound thoroughly — wash with clean water and soap for at least 5 minutes.
2. Remove visible dirt, rust, or foreign material by flushing with water. Do not scrub deeply.
3. Do NOT close or bandage the wound tightly — tetanus thrives in sealed wounds without oxygen.
4. Go to a health worker or hospital for:
   - Tetanus toxoid vaccination booster (if last dose was more than 5 years ago)
   - Tetanus immunoglobulin (TIG) injection if wound is severe and person has never been vaccinated
   - Antibiotic prescription (metronidazole or penicillin — only prescribed by medical staff)
5. Continue monitoring for symptoms for up to 21 days.

If tetanus symptoms have already appeared:
1. Go to hospital immediately — do not attempt any home treatment.
2. Keep the patient calm and in a quiet, dark area — spasms are triggered by light, noise, and touch.
3. Do not give food or water if swallowing is impaired.

DO NOT:
- Do not close a high-risk wound tightly — tetanus bacteria produce toxin in low-oxygen (sealed) environments.
- Do not attempt to treat tetanus symptoms at home — hospitalization and antitoxin are required.
- Do not delay seeking a tetanus booster after any dirty wound — vaccine protection takes 48 hours to activate.

PREVENTION — PROTECT NEWBORNS:
- Deliver babies with a clean blade to cut the cord.
- Keep the umbilical cord stump clean and dry — do not apply mud, ash, or animal dung.
- Pregnant women should receive tetanus vaccination if not previously immunized.
