Animal and Human Bites — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: animal bite, dog bite, cat bite, human bite, rat bite

RECOGNIZING AN INFECTED BITE:
Fresh bite: puncture marks or torn skin, bleeding, bruising around the wound.
Signs of infection developing (usually 24–72 hours after bite):
- Increasing redness spreading beyond the wound edges
- Swelling and warmth around the bite
- Pus or discharge leaking from the wound
- Wound smells unpleasant
- Patient has a high temperature or feels feverish and shivery
- Red streak spreading up the arm or leg from the bite site (lymphangitis — urgent sign)

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Go to hospital or a doctor without delay if:
- The bite is deep, gaping, or will not stop bleeding
- There are possible teeth, hair, or dirt fragments inside the wound
- The bite is on the hands, feet, face, or head — these areas are higher infection risk
- The bite was from a human — human mouth bacteria cause severe infections
- The patient has not had a tetanus vaccine in the last 5–10 years
- The bite was from a stray dog, wild animal, bat, or any animal behaving abnormally — rabies risk
- Signs of infection are present (see above)
- A red streak is spreading from the wound up the limb — this is an emergency

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:
1. Wash the wound thoroughly with soap and clean running water for at least 5 minutes. Do not rush this step — thorough washing is the most important thing you can do to prevent infection.
2. Gently squeeze the wound to encourage a small amount of bleeding — this helps flush out bacteria. Do not squeeze a heavily bleeding wound.
3. If bleeding heavily, apply firm continuous pressure with a clean cloth or dressing until bleeding slows.
4. Once bleeding is controlled, rinse again with clean water and gently pat dry.
5. Cover with a clean bandage or dressing.
6. Give paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain and swelling — children under 16 should not take aspirin.
7. Go to a doctor or hospital for assessment — most animal bites need antibiotic treatment and a tetanus booster check.

DO NOT:
- Do not close the wound tightly with tape or stitches before medical assessment — sealing a bite wound traps bacteria and causes abscess formation.
- Do not apply herbal remedies, raw egg, or any substance to the wound.
- Do not ignore a bite that appears infected — infections from bites spread rapidly and can reach the bone.
- Do not delay seeking care for bites on the hand — hand infections are particularly dangerous and can cause permanent disability.

PREVENTION:
- Do not approach unfamiliar animals, especially stray dogs and cats, during and after disasters — animals become stressed and aggressive during floods and earthquakes.
- Wear rubber boots and thick clothing when moving through flood-affected areas where rats are present.
- Rat bites are common during floods when rodents are displaced — check dark corners, bedding, and stored food carefully.
- If bitten by any animal during a flood, mention the flood exposure to the treating doctor — leptospirosis and other flood-related infections are transmitted through rat bites and scratches.
