Malaria — Recognition, Prevention and First Aid

RECOGNIZING MALARIA:
Symptoms appear 10–15 days after a mosquito bite.
- Fever with chills — often comes in cycles (every 1–3 days)
- Severe headache
- Muscle aches and fatigue
- Nausea, vomiting
- Sweating after fever peak

SEVERE MALARIA — GO TO HOSPITAL IMMEDIATELY:
- High fever with confusion or difficulty waking
- Multiple seizures or convulsions
- Difficulty breathing or rapid breathing
- Dark or bloody urine ("blackwater fever")
- Jaundice (yellowing of eyes and skin)
- Extreme weakness — cannot stand or sit
- Child unable to drink or breastfeed
Severe malaria can kill within 24 hours without treatment. Do not delay.

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING FOR MEDICAL CARE:
1. Give paracetamol for high fever — follow dosing on the packet.
2. Keep the patient cool with a damp cloth on the forehead — do not over-bundle.
3. Encourage fluids: water, ORS, or clear soup.
4. If prescribed an artemisinin-based treatment (e.g., Coartem) by a health worker, give the full 3-day course — stopping early causes relapse and drug resistance.
5. Do NOT give aspirin to children with fever.
6. Keep patient resting — avoid unnecessary movement in severe cases.

HIGH-RISK GROUPS — MONITOR CLOSELY:
- Children under 5 years: check for fast breathing, seizures, inability to drink
- Pregnant women: malaria during pregnancy causes premature delivery and low birth weight — seek care at first fever
- People with HIV: higher risk of severe illness

PREVENTION:
1. Sleep under an insecticide-treated mosquito net every night — even indoors.
2. Use mosquito repellent containing DEET on exposed skin after dusk.
3. Wear long sleeves and trousers in the evening and at night.
4. Close windows and doors at dusk, or use window screens.
5. Do not allow water to collect in open containers, tyres, or pots — mosquitoes breed in standing water.
6. If you have a fever in a malaria area — get tested and treated. Do not wait.
