Broken Arm, Wrist, or Collarbone — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: broken arm, broken wrist, fractured arm, broken collarbone, arm fracture

RECOGNIZING A BROKEN ARM OR WRIST:
Suspect a fracture if, after an injury, the arm or wrist:
- Is painful, especially when moved or touched at one specific point
- Is visibly swollen or bruised
- Looks bent, deformed, or out of the normal shape
- Cannot be moved normally, or movement causes severe pain
- Has gone numb or tingly
- Has a bone visible through the skin (open fracture — emergency)
It can be difficult to tell a broken wrist from a bad sprain — treat it as a fracture if in doubt.

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Go to hospital for all suspected broken arms. Seek help immediately if:
- The bone is visible through the skin (open fracture)
- The arm below the injury is cold, pale, blue, or numb — blood supply may be cut off
- The patient is a child — children's bones fracture differently and need proper assessment
- There is severe swelling or the arm has changed shape significantly
- The patient cannot feel or move their fingers
- The injury was caused by a high-force event (fall from height, building collapse, vehicle accident)

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:
1. Support the injured arm in the most comfortable position the patient can hold it. Do not try to straighten it.
2. Make a sling: fold a large cloth (scarf, dupatta, shirt) into a triangle. Slide it under the arm so the point is at the elbow and the two ends go around the neck. Tie it so the hand is slightly higher than the elbow. The arm should rest across the chest.
3. If a sling is not available, support the arm against the chest with the patient's other hand, or pin the sleeve of their shirt to their chest clothing.
4. Apply a cold pack or a bag of ice wrapped in cloth to the injury for up to 20 minutes — this reduces swelling and pain. Do not apply ice directly to skin.
5. Remove any rings, bracelets, or watches from the injured arm and hand immediately — swelling will make removal impossible later.
6. Give paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain. Do not give aspirin to children under 16.
7. Do not give food or drink — surgery under anaesthesia may be needed.

DO NOT:
- Do not try to straighten the arm or push the bone back into position.
- Do not wrap the arm tightly — bandages that are too tight cut off circulation.
- Do not allow the patient to use the injured arm for any weight-bearing activity.
- Do not apply heat (hot water bottle, warm compress) — heat increases swelling.

PREVENTION:
- In disaster settings, watch for loose rubble, unstable floors, and slippery surfaces — falls are the most common cause of arm fractures.
- Support elderly people when moving over uneven ground — their bones fracture more easily.
