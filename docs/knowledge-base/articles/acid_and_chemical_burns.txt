Acid and Chemical Burns — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: acid burn, chemical burn, caustic burn

RECOGNIZING ACID AND CHEMICAL BURNS:
- Skin that appears red, blistered, or has turned white or dark (dead tissue)
- Intense burning pain at the site of contact
- Swelling around the affected area
- If eyes are exposed: intense eye pain, blurred vision, inability to open the eye
- If inhaled: coughing, choking, difficulty breathing, burning sensation in throat
- Clothing may be stained, dissolved, or still wet with the chemical

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Go to hospital immediately if:
- The burn is larger than the palm of the hand
- The burn is on the face, eyes, hands, feet, or genitals
- The patient is having difficulty breathing (inhaled fumes)
- The patient swallowed the chemical
- The burn was caused by a concentrated industrial chemical or battery acid
- Pain is severe or the skin has turned white, black, or charred
All chemical burns should be assessed in hospital even if they appear minor — tissue damage may continue after first aid.

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:
1. Protect yourself first — wear gloves if available before touching the patient or their clothing.
2. Remove any contaminated clothing and jewellery from the affected area — cut clothing away rather than pulling it over the head if the face or neck is affected.
3. If a dry chemical powder: brush it off the skin carefully before adding water — water can activate some dry chemicals and worsen the burn.
4. Flood the burned area with large amounts of cool or lukewarm running water for at least 20 minutes without stopping — do not use ice or ice-cold water.
5. While washing, remove any remaining contaminated clothing from the affected area.
6. If the eyes are affected: hold the eye open and flush with clean running water for at least 20 minutes. Remove contact lenses before flushing if possible.
7. Keep the patient as still and calm as possible. Do not rub the burned area.
8. Cover loosely with a clean non-fluffy cloth or sterile dressing after washing — do not use fluffy cotton wool.
9. Give paracetamol for pain while waiting for hospital care.

DO NOT:
- Do not apply creams, ointments, toothpaste, butter, or any substance to the burn — these trap the chemical and cause further damage.
- Do not try to neutralize an acid burn with an alkali or vice versa — this generates heat and worsens the burn.
- Do not rub or wipe the burned skin.
- Do not use ice-cold water — it causes additional tissue damage and hypothermia.
- Do not eat or drink if the patient swallowed the chemical.

PREVENTION:
- Store all household chemicals (bleach, drain cleaner, battery acid) in locked, labelled containers out of children's reach.
- Wear protective gloves and eye protection when handling any cleaning chemicals or industrial substances.
- In flood-affected areas, be aware that floodwater may contain industrial chemicals from submerged factories or storage facilities.
