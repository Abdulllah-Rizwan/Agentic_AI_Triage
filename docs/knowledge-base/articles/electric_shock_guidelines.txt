Electric Shock — First Aid Guide

TOPIC: electric shock, electrocution, bijli lagni, power line, flood electricity

RECOGNIZING ELECTRIC SHOCK INJURIES:
- Entry wound where current entered body (may appear as a small burn)
- Exit wound where current left (often on foot or hand touching the ground)
- Do not judge severity by visible wound size — massive internal damage can occur between entry and exit points
- Burns to the skin at entry and exit points
- Muscle cramps or tetanic contraction (muscles locked in contraction)
- Loss of consciousness, confusion
- Cardiac arrest — current disrupts heart rhythm
- Spinal injury if the person fell or was thrown by the shock

Warning signs after the incident (even if person seems fine):
- Palpitations or irregular heartbeat
- Chest pain
- Dizziness or fainting

WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE:
Every person who has received a significant electric shock must go to hospital — even if they feel well and have no visible injuries. Delayed cardiac arrhythmias and internal organ damage can be fatal hours after the incident.

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING:
Scene safety first — do not touch the person until power is confirmed off:
1. Do not touch a person in contact with an electrical source — you will receive the shock.
2. Turn off electricity at the main switch or circuit breaker.
3. If you cannot turn off the supply: use a dry non-conducting object (dry wooden plank, dry rope, rubber mat) to push the person away from the source. Never use anything wet or metal.
4. Only approach when you are certain the power is off.

After making the scene safe:
1. Check responsiveness and breathing.
2. If unresponsive and not breathing: begin CPR immediately. Call Rescue 1122 or Edhi 115.
3. If conscious: lay them down and monitor for deterioration. Check for burns.
4. Cool any visible burns with cool running water for 20 minutes. Cover loosely with clean non-fluffy cloth.
5. If the person fell or was thrown by the shock: assume spinal injury. Do not move the neck or spine. Immobilize the head.
6. Keep the patient lying down and calm until emergency help arrives.

DO NOT:
- Do not touch a person in contact with an electrical source — you will be electrocuted too.
- Do not enter water where electrical equipment is submerged.
- Do not approach downed power lines — stay at least 10 metres away even if they appear inactive.
- Do not remove clothing stuck to electrical burns.
- Do not judge injury as minor because entry and exit wounds are small.

PREVENTING ELECTROCUTION DURING FLOODS:
- Do not turn on electricity in a flooded home. Have all wiring, switches, and appliances checked by a qualified electrician before restoring power.
- Do not use petrol or diesel generators indoors — they produce carbon monoxide which causes rapid death in enclosed spaces.
- Never use electrical equipment while standing in water.
- If a power line is down in your area, contact the electricity authority (LESCO, HESCO, PESCO) and keep people away from the entire area.
