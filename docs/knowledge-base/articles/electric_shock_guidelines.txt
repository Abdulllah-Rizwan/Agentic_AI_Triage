Electric Shock — Emergency First Aid

Electric shock injuries are a significant cause of death during and after floods in Pakistan. Floods submerge electrical infrastructure, bring down power lines, and create hidden electrocution hazards in floodwater. Even domestic voltage (220V in Pakistan) can kill. Higher-voltage lines cause massive internal injuries, deep burns, and cardiac arrest.

DANGERS SPECIFIC TO FLOODS

Submerged electrical equipment: appliances, wiring, and generators in floodwater can electrify the water.
Downed power lines: fallen high-voltage lines may not be visibly sparking but remain live. Water conducts electricity from a downed line for a wide radius.
Returning home: people returning to flooded homes may turn on appliances before checking electrical systems — this is a leading cause of post-flood electrocution deaths.

SAFETY RULE: DO NOT TOUCH

Do not touch a person who is in contact with an electrical source — you will receive the shock through them.
Do not enter water where electrical equipment is submerged or where a power line is down.
Do not approach a downed power line. Even if it appears inactive, assume it is live. Stay at least 10 metres away.

MAKING THE SCENE SAFE — BEFORE TOUCHING THE VICTIM

Turn off the electrical source at the main switch or circuit breaker if safe to do so.
If you cannot turn off the supply, do not touch the victim with your hands. Use a dry non-conducting object to push the person away from the source: a dry wooden plank, dry rope, rubber mat, or plastic object. Never use anything wet or metal.
Only approach when you are certain the power is off.

IMMEDIATE ASSESSMENT AFTER MAKING SCENE SAFE

Electric shock can cause cardiac arrest instantly — the electrical current disrupts heart rhythm. Check immediately:
Is the person breathing? Look, listen, and feel for 10 seconds.
Is the person conscious and responsive?

If unresponsive and not breathing: Begin CPR immediately. Call Rescue 1122 or Edhi 115.

If conscious: lay them down, check for burns (entry and exit wounds), and monitor for deterioration.

BURNS FROM ELECTRIC SHOCK

Electrical burns have an entry wound (where current entered the body) and an exit wound (where it left). There is severe internal damage between these two points that may not be visible.

Do not judge injury severity by the size of the visible burn — a small entry wound can conceal massive internal damage.
Cool visible burns with cool running water for 20 minutes.
Cover burns loosely with a clean non-fluffy cloth.
Do not remove clothing stuck to burned skin.
All electrical burns require hospital assessment — internal organ damage, kidney failure from muscle breakdown, and delayed cardiac arrhythmias can occur hours after the injury.

HEART PROBLEMS AFTER ELECTRIC SHOCK

Electrical current can cause abnormal heart rhythms (arrhythmias) that may not cause symptoms immediately but can be fatal hours after the incident. Every person who has received a significant electric shock must be monitored in hospital — even if they feel well and have no visible injuries.

Warning signs: palpitations, chest pain, dizziness, fainting.

SPINAL INJURY FROM ELECTRIC SHOCK

The violent muscle contraction caused by an electric shock can fracture the spine. If a person has fallen or been thrown by a shock:
Assume a spinal injury.
Do not move the neck or spine.
Immobilize the head in the position found and wait for emergency help.

ACTIONS AFTER FLOODS — PREVENTING ELECTROCUTION

Do not turn on electricity in a flooded home. Have all wiring, switches, and appliances checked by a qualified electrician before restoring power.
Do not use petrol or diesel generators indoors — they produce carbon monoxide which causes rapid death in enclosed spaces.
Never use electrical equipment while standing in water.
If a power line is down in your area, contact the electricity authority (LESCO, HESCO, PESCO) and keep people away from the entire area.
